# Neurologic-mechanics interaction rationale

The neurologic-mechanics indicator combines cerebral palsy, muscular
dystrophies/myopathies, selected movement disorders, and selected scoliosis
families. It is a diagnosis-based study grouping, not a measurement of
respiratory-muscle weakness, restrictive lung physiology, or disease severity.

Respiratory-muscle weakness, impaired cough or bulbar function, and altered
chest-wall mechanics provide a plausible reason for respiratory support and
recovery to differ in some children with these conditions. Pediatric
neuromuscular guidance describes heterogeneous respiratory impairment, and
neuromuscular respiratory-management guidance emphasizes vital capacity,
respiratory-muscle strength, and cough assessment.

This rationale does not directly establish an interaction between the study
indicator and size-normalized tidal volume for mortality or successful
liberation. The reviewed sources do not validate the combined indicator or
favor PBW over PFVC normalization.

The completed analysis evaluated the modifier separately with four tidal-volume
exposures for 60-day mortality and 28-day Fine–Gray liberation. It remained
exploratory, used unadjusted P values across related specifications, and had
limited modifier-positive mortality events. It should not be described
retrospectively as confirmatory or as evidence that increasing tidal volume
improves outcomes.

This evidence note was prepared after the exploratory models were implemented
and reviewed. It documents the clinical rationale and its limitations; it does
not change the models, cohort, or interpretation policy.
